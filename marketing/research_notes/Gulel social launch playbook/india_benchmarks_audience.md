## 1. Paid media benchmarks in India (CPM, CPC, CTR, CPI, cost per registration/purchase) for entertainment, OTT and short-drama apps, 2024–2026

### Takeaway
India is still one of the cheapest places in the world to buy installs. Reported Android CPIs for entertainment apps are about ₹10–45, and iOS runs 2–4x higher. Meta CPMs run about ₹20–150 depending on the campaign objective, with Reels 25–40% cheaper than Feed. Festive CPMs (Oct–Nov) inflate 30–50%. The real constraint for micro-drama apps is paid conversion, not install cost. Indian peers spend more on marketing than they earn: Kuku FM spent ₹285 Cr on marketing against ₹242 Cr of revenue in FY25. Industry panels describe the category as "90% of budget goes to marketing".

### Inferences
- **Planning CPI for Gulel (Android, Hindi, Tier 2/3 geos):** use ₹15–30 as the base case for Meta/Google app-install campaigns in non-festive weeks and ₹25–45 in festive weeks (+30–50% CPM). Keep iOS to ≤5% of budget, or skip it at launch; expected iOS CPI is ₹70–150. This combines the vendor CPI ranges with the festive CPM uplift and should be validated in a 2–3 week test.
- **CTR is the lever.** Pocket FM's claim of a 0.25pp CTR gain cutting CAC ~30% suggests Gulel should budget for high creative volume: dozens of cutdowns per title, rotated weekly. The category norm is "thousands of trailer variations".
- **Marketing intensity:** Kuku FM spent ₹1.18 of marketing per ₹1 of revenue in FY25 (285/242). Treat year-1 marketing ≥ revenue as normal for the category, not a failure signal.
- Illustrative cost per first purchase: if 2–5% of installs convert to a first purchase (assumed; see Q2 gaps), a ₹20 CPI implies roughly **₹400–1,000 per first payer**. That is comparable to a year of ARPPU (~₹1,300; see Q2). This is a modelling assumption, not a sourced benchmark.
- Hindi-language creative may also lower CPMs (vendor claim of 40–60%). Worth an A/B test (Devanagari vs Hinglish vs English) in week 1.

## 2. Monetisation benchmarks: payer conversion, ARPPU, price points, trials, UPI Autopay, store vs web billing

### Takeaway
India micro-drama monetises at roughly $15 (≈₹1,300) per paying user per year, against ~$35 for classic OTT. Payment runs on ₹1–₹2 trials that roll into UPI AutoPay subscriptions: 70–80% of micro-drama subscribers authorise mandates, and up to 60% would drop without them. Kuku TV (₹399/quarter, ₹899/year) and free, ad-supported JioHotstar Tadka set the price anchors Gulel's ₹149 coin pack will be judged against. The Google Play alternative-billing discount is only 4 points, so web/UPI checkout carries a large fee advantage. The new UPI MDR (from 15 Oct 2026) does not touch transactions ≤ ₹2,000 or AutoPay mandates.

There are three options: Play Billing; an alternative billing system (cards, wallets, net banking, **UPI**); or a consumption-only app. **If the user picks alternative billing, the service fee drops by only 4 percentage points.** — [Google Play Console Help: India billing changes](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13306652?hl=en); [Play Console Help: UCB](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en); [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/393666/google-play-in-app-user-choice-billing-india.html)
- Under UCB, commission is effectively **11–26%** after the 4% rebate. The CCI said the **10–30% service fee** still hinders developers and **ordered a probe of UCB in March 2024**. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/googles-10-30-service-fee-on-app-purchase-hindering-app-developers-cci-tells-nclat/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/05/google-play-payments-policy-india-compliant-with-cci-order)

### Inferences
- **Payer ratio benchmarks (derived):** Lumikai's 17M payers / 100M MAU ≈ **17% of MAU paying**. This is high and likely inflated by ₹1–₹2 trial subscribers. 17M payers / 450M cumulative downloads ≈ **3.8% of all-time installs**. For a new app, plan on **2–5% of installs → first purchase** in month 1 (base 3%). Treat 10% as the ceiling reported by STAGE, whose login→paid rate was helped by a ₹1 trial.
- **ARPPU:** $15/yr ≈ **₹1,300/yr ≈ ₹110/month per payer** (at ₹87/$). STAGE's ₹111 Cr / 4.4M payers ≈ ₹250 per payer per year (a crude lower bound, since payers accumulated through the year).
- **Gulel price-anchor problem:** ₹149 = 100 coins = ~10 episodes → **~₹15/episode**. Finishing a 60–80-episode title fully paid costs ~₹900–1,200, which equals **a full year of Kuku TV (₹899)**. Tadka is free. Recommendations: (a) make the VIP pass the hero offer, priced against ₹399/quarter and ₹899/year; (b) add a ₹1–₹2 trial or a ₹9–₹49 sachet as the first-purchase bridge; (c) lean on rewarded ads (+5 coins) and daily free unlocks, similar to Pocket FM's 30 free minutes per show per day, for non-payers.
- **Web/UPI checkout beats Play Billing on margin:** the UCB rebate is only 4 pts (11–26% vs 15–30%). A web checkout on thegulel.com with UPI AutoPay would carry only payment-gateway fees (typically ~2%; not sourced here, verify with PG). The Kuku TV/Story TV store-revenue gap suggests Indian peers already route most payments outside store billing.
- The UPI MDR is **irrelevant for Gulel's ₹149 packs and for AutoPay VIP renewals**. It only matters for any single purchase above ₹2,000.
- **Mandate churn risk:** 20M+ mandates are cancelled monthly for insufficient balance. Schedule VIP renewals around the salary and credit cycle (first days of the month) and build retry logic.

### Gaps
- **No sourced payer conversion rate for coin-based (non-subscription) Indian short-drama apps.** No sourced distribution of first-purchase price points (₹9/₹19/₹49/₹99/₹149) was found either; search budget exhausted.
- No trial-to-paid conversion rate for ₹1/₹2 trials (Kuku TV, STAGE) in 2025–26.
- No verified figure for the conversion uplift from UPI vs cards. FICCI-EY's "60% would drop without AutoPay" is the closest proxy.
- Apple App Store India commission and alternative payment rules were not researched.
- Market-size conflict (Lumikai $300M vs FICCI-EY ₹650 Cr) is unresolved.
