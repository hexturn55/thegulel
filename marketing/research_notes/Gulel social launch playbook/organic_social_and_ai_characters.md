## Q1. What works for character/in-world accounts and virtual influencers (case studies, growth, engagement, cadence, formats, monetisation, pitfalls)

### Takeaway
The two best-documented Indian virtual influencers are Kyra (FUTR Studios) and Naina (Avtr Meta Labs). Both grew to roughly 100k–320k Instagram followers within 1–2 years. They did it with lifestyle, fashion, fitness, dance and trend-led Reels, then monetised through mainstream brand deals (Amazon Prime Video, boAt, Nykaa, Puma, Pepsi). Neither is a fiction/series character, so Gulel's model is closer to "TV-character accounts" than to lifestyle virtual influencers. From Sept 2026, Instagram also penalises the reach of AI-human accounts that are not labelled (see Q4).

### Inferences
- Kyra and Naina show that an Indian audience will follow a clearly artificial persona at meaningful scale. But their playbook is lifestyle/brand-endorsement, not narrative. Gulel's characters (Pishachini the healer-witch, CEO hero, substitute bride, mafia lord) should borrow their trend-riding Reel formats (dance/trend audio, "day in my life", fashion/look reveals) *in character*. Their CTA, though, should be "watch what happened in Episode 1", not brand endorsement.
- Gulel's characters are story IP first. The right comparison set is TV/film character accounts and mascot-led brand accounts (Duolingo-style). This research could not verify that set (see Gaps).
- Monetisation: character accounts that build a following could later carry brand integrations. Under ASCI (Q4), every such post would need a double disclosure: the ad label plus an "AI/virtual, not a real human" label.

## Q2. Platform-by-platform organic playbooks for India, 2025–2026 (Instagram, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, Threads, WhatsApp Channels, Moj, Josh, ShareChat, Snapchat, Telegram)

- YouTube: from May 2026, the AI label on Shorts appears as an overlay on the video during playback. On long-form it sits directly below the player, above the description. YouTube says labels do not by themselves reduce recommendations or monetisation — [TechCrunch, 27 May 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/); [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-ai-video-labels-automatic-detection-1236758865/); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/)

### Inferences
- **Instagram:** run the brand account plus separate character accounts, and use **Collab posts** (brand + character co-authored) for episode clips instead of posting the same clip on several accounts. Each character account should post *character-native* material (monologues, "POV" confessions, in-world diary entries, trend formats in character) that differs from the brand's trailer cuts.
- **YouTube:** the on-Shorts AI overlay is unavoidable for photorealistic Seedance/Kling output. Creative should embrace it (e.g., "an AI-original series" as brand positioning) rather than fight it.
- **Facebook** (large tier-2/3 Hindi reach, per the brief's premise): the July 2025 unoriginal-content rules mean Gulel should post first-party originals from its own Page. It should avoid re-uploading the same clips across many Pages or fan-page networks.

## Q3. Series-clip distribution: full pilots natively vs trailers/cliffhangers; avoiding reused/duplicate-content penalties

### Takeaway
All three major platforms now penalise duplicate or reposted content, and YouTube also penalises templated, mass-produced content. Instagram (Reels since mid-2024; photos and carousels since April 2026) swaps in the "original" when it detects identical content and stops recommending aggregator-style accounts. Facebook (July 2025) cuts distribution and monetisation for unoriginal reposts. YouTube (15 Jul 2025) renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" to cover templated, mass-produced videos. For a multi-account AI-series operation, the risk is not stealing others' content. It is looking like a template farm, or posting identical clips across Gulel's own accounts.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram**
- On 30 Apr 2024, Adam Mosseri said Instagram would stop recommending aggregator content to non-followers. When it detects two identical pieces of content it would replace reposted Reels with the "original" in recommendations and label the reposting account with credit to the original. Rollout was from summer 2024 — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Business Wonderland](https://businesswonderland.co.uk/instagram-algorithm-update-april-2024-according-to-instagrams-adam-mosseri/)
- April 2026 expansion: accounts that "primarily share content they didn't create, or haven't meaningfully transformed" are no longer recommended to non-followers. This now covers photos and carousels, not just Reels. Watermarks, minor crops and basic reposts do not count as original; "materially edited" or transformative work does. Followers' feeds are unaffected — [PetaPixel, 30 Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [MWM](https://mwm.ai/articles/instagram-algorithm-update-in-april-2026-prioritizes-original-content)

**Facebook**
- Meta blog of 14 Jul 2025: crackdown on "unoriginal content", defined as reusing another creator's work repeatedly without credit, as distinct from adding commentary, reaction or perspective. Meta removed about 10M profiles impersonating large creators and actioned about 500,000 accounts in H1 2025 for spammy behaviour. Penalties: reduced distribution and loss of monetisation. Meta is also testing tools that link duplicates back to originals — [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-introduces-stronger-measures-against-unoriginal-content-creators/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/16/facebook-to-fight-unoriginal-content/)
- March 2026: Facebook made it easier for creators to report impersonators (headline only; details not retrieved) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/13/facebook-makes-it-easier-for-creators-to-report-impersonators/)

**YouTube**
- On 15 Jul 2025, the YPP policy renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". It covers content that is "repetitive or mass-produced", follows a template with minimal variation, is easy to reproduce at scale, and lacks clear author input. Examples: narrated-story channels with only superficial differences between videos, and slideshow channels with identical narration. YouTube says this was always ineligible for monetisation — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [YouTube Help – channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- LOW-QUALITY SOURCE, treat as unverified: an aggregator claims a three-strike system for mass-produced AI videos (warning, then 90-day suspension, then permanent YPP removal) and mass channel suspensions in Jan 2026. No primary confirmation was found — [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Don't cross-post identical clips across the brand account and the four character accounts.** On Instagram, one Collab post (brand + character co-authors) gets a single "original" with shared reach. On Facebook and YouTube, post each clip once from the canonical Gulel handle. Character accounts should carry differentiated, in-character edits: new VO, a POV reframe, a reaction or "confession" cut, a different hook. These are "materially edited" and add author input.
- **Full pilots vs cliffhanger cuts:** no data was found. The originality rules don't forbid native full episodes, since Gulel owns them. A plausible split: full pilot natively on the YouTube brand channel (long-form, or a Shorts series of 60–180s parts linked with "related video"). On Instagram/Facebook, use cliffhanger cuts that end on an unresolved beat, with the CTA to the full episode. Test this; it is not evidence-backed.
- **Templated AI output risk on YouTube:** a channel of near-identical AI clips with the same structure, VO and captions could match "inauthentic content" (template, minimal variation, no author input). Vary formats and add behind-the-scenes or creator commentary, recaps and fan-theory responses to show human authorship.

## Q4. Disclosure and labelling rules for AI-generated content and virtual influencers (India primary), status as of Sept 2026

### Takeaway
**IN FORCE (India, law):** the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 120(E), notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026) cover "synthetically generated information" (SGI).
- SGI must be prominently labelled and carry permanent provenance metadata or a unique identifier that users cannot strip.
- Significant social media intermediaries (SSMIs, over 50 lakh users) must collect a user declaration of SGI before publication, verify it technically, and display a label.
- The October 2025 draft's fixed "10% of screen / first 10% of audio" label size was reportedly dropped in the final rules.
- Takedown for flagged unlawful content falls from 36 hours to 3 hours.

**IN FORCE (self-regulatory):** ASCI's influencer guidelines already require virtual influencers to disclose upfront and prominently that the audience is not dealing with a real human.

**DRAFT:** ASCI's AI-labelling guidelines for advertising (8 May 2026; comments closed 13 Jun 2026; no evidence of finalisation found). They put AI likeness/voice and virtual influencers in a "medium risk / mandatory label" tier.

**Platforms, IN FORCE:**
- Meta requires self-disclosure for photorealistic AI video and realistic audio. Instagram's "AI-generated profile" label comes with reach limits for undisclosed AI-human accounts (announced 31 Aug 2026).
- YouTube requires the altered/synthetic disclosure in Studio. Since May 2026 there is a visible on-Shorts overlay label and automatic labelling of undisclosed photorealistic AI. Labels are permanent where C2PA says fully AI or YouTube's own AI tools were used.

Gulel's photorealistic Seedance/Kling/Nano Banana output with human-looking characters and ElevenLabs voices falls squarely inside all of these.

### Cited Findings
**A. India: IT Amendment Rules 2026 (SGI). Status: IN FORCE since 20 Feb 2026**
- Notified as G.S.R. 120(E) on 10 Feb 2026, in force from 20 Feb 2026. Amends the IT Rules 2021 to bring deepfakes and synthetic media into intermediary due diligence — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/government-notifies-information-technology-amendment-rules-2026)
- **Definition of SGI:** "audio, visual or audio-visual information which is artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified or altered using a computer resource, in a manner that such information appears to be real, authentic or true and depicts or portrays any individual or event in a manner that is, or is likely to be perceived as indistinguishable from a natural person or real-world event" — [Khaitan & Co ERGO (16 Feb 2026)](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-02/ERGO%20-%20IT%20Rules%20Amendment%20-%2016%20February%202026.pdf); [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/); [Cyril Amarchand summary (11 Feb 2026)](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Client-Alert-Summary-on-SGI-Amendments.pdf)
- **Exclusions (good-faith uses):** (a) text-only content; (b) routine or good-faith editing, including formatting and technical corrections, that does not materially distort or misrepresent the substance, context or meaning; (c) preparation of documents; (d) tools that improve accessibility, clarity, quality, translation, description, searchability or discoverability without manipulating any material part of the content — [ANM Global](https://anmglobal.net/regulating-synthetic-content-key-implications-of-the-it-rules-amendment-2026/); [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/)
- **Labelling and provenance:** SGI "shall be prominently labelled and embedded with permanent metadata or appropriate technical provenance mechanism including unique identifier". Intermediaries must not enable "modification, suppression or removal of the label, permanent metadata" — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=adb2714c-1188-43e3-b62b-eb6649727f6e); [Kan and Krishme](https://kankrishme.com/indias-2026-it-rules-amendment-regulating-ai-generated-content-and-accelerating-compliance/)
- **SSMI duties (Rule 4(1A)), for SSMIs with over 50 lakh users.** Before upload or publication, the SSMI must:
  1. obtain a user declaration on whether content is SGI;
  2. deploy "reasonable and appropriate technical measures", including automated tools, to verify the declaration;
  3. display a clear and prominent label where content is confirmed as SGI.

  An SSMI that "knowingly permits, promotes or fails to act upon" SGI in contravention is deemed to have failed due diligence, which puts safe harbour at risk — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1749112/the-2026-it-amendment-rules-a-new-era-of-informatics-accountability); [PJ Law](https://pjlaw.in/blogs/it-rules-amendment-2026-ai-generated-content-deepfake-compliance/); [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-platforms-users-meity-it-rules-synthetic-media/)
- **Takedown:** the deadline to remove flagged unlawful content is cut from 36 hours to 3 hours — [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [eCorpIT](https://ecorpit.com/india-it-rules-2026-deepfake-takedown-ai-labelling/)
- **Draft vs final.** MeitY released the draft on 22 Oct 2025. It proposed that labels cover at least 10% of the visual display area, or play during the first 10% of an audio clip's duration — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/india's-draft-it-rules-2025-meity-proposes-mandatory-labelling-of-ai-generated-content); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1704380/indias-draft-it-rules-2025-meity-proposes-mandatory-labelling-of-ai-generated-content); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/meity-tightens-it-rules-mandates-labelling-of-ai-generated-content-on-social-media-82926.htm); [MeitY explanatory note (22 Oct 2025)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/8e40cdd134cd92dd783a37556428c370.pdf); [MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf). A search summary citing these sources, including [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn), says the fixed 10% requirement was dropped from the final rules in favour of "clearly and prominently labelled" with no fixed size. A second search summary found no percentage in the final-rule sources, which is consistent. *Moderate confidence: the final Gazette text was not read directly.*
- **Critique:** the Internet Freedom Foundation argues the 2026 rules "contradict their purpose" (details not retrieved) — [IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026-contradict-their-purpose/)

**B. ASCI (self-regulatory advertising code)**
- **Virtual influencers, IN FORCE.** ASCI's Guidelines for Influencer Advertising in Digital Media define virtual influencers as "fictional computer-generated 'people' or avatars who have the realistic characteristics, features and personalities of humans, and behave similarly as influencers". A virtual influencer "must additionally disclose to consumers that they are not interacting with a real human being", and this disclosure must be "upfront and prominent" — [ASCI guidelines PDF (2023 version)](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/GUIDELINES-FOR-INFLUENCER-ADVERTISING-IN-DIGITAL-MEDIA.pdf); [ASCI guidelines PDF (2024 version)](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/ASCI-Guidelines-Influencer-Advertising-In-Digital-Media-1.pdf); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/social-media-vs-reality-check-asci-s-new-influencer-guidelines)
- **General placement rules** under the same guidelines: a disclosure must be understood by an average consumer and must be upfront, so users should not have to click "see more". Blanket disclosures such as "link in bio" are inadequate — [ASCI guidelines PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/GUIDELINES-FOR-INFLUENCER-ADVERTISING-IN-DIGITAL-MEDIA.pdf); [ASCI Social disclosure tool](https://www.ascionline.in/social/tools/)
- **AI in advertising, DRAFT (not final as of the latest source found).** "Draft Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of AI-Generated Content in Advertising", published 8 May 2026 and aligned with the IT Amendment Rules 2026. Comments were invited until 13 Jun 2026, "after which the guideline finalisation process will begin". The draft aims for transparency without "consumer label fatigue" — [ASCI draft PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AI-Draft-Guidelines-1.pdf); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=eea2c4b2-6d46-4036-8869-d19aa3217c1c); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/advertising-marketing-branding/1792778/asci-issues-draft-guidelines-for-responsible-labelling-of-ai-generated-content-in-advertising); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-advocates-risk-based-approach-for-synthetically-generated-content-in-advertising-154586.html); [medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/asci-releases-draft-guidelines-for-responsible-labelling-of-ai-generated-advertising-content/)
  - **Risk tiers:**
    - High risk: prohibited. Illegal, rights-infringing or misleading content, or content in breach of the ASCI Code.
    - Medium risk: mandatory label. AI use "can materially influence" consumer decisions, so omitting disclosure would make the ad misleading.
    - Low risk: no label required.

    — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=733f8311-c492-48bf-8a26-76f535c9247b); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/ai-ads-face-asci-test-as-draft-rules-target-deepfakes-misleading-claims-126051201030_1.html)
  - **Prescribed label wording:** "Audio/Video created using AI" or "Audio/Video enhanced using AI", or any alternative wording that sufficiently informs the consumer — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=eea2c4b2-6d46-4036-8869-d19aa3217c1c); [medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/asci-releases-draft-guidelines-for-responsible-labelling-of-ai-generated-advertising-content/)
  - Virtual influencers and AI-generated likeness or voice replication fall in the medium-risk (mandatory-label) tier. This applies even with the depicted person's consent — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/ai-ads-face-asci-test-as-draft-rules-target-deepfakes-misleading-claims-126051201030_1.html); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=733f8311-c492-48bf-8a26-76f535c9247b)
  - *Secondary interpretation, not confirmed in ASCI text:* every promotional post by a virtual influencer needs both the AI/virtual disclosure and the paid-content disclosure, with the AI label visible on the content itself rather than buried in the caption — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers); [IdentityKit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/asci-guidelines-influencer-disclosure-india-2026)
  - Industry commentary frames the draft as "the beginning of a new compliance era" — [BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/advertising/ascis-ai-disclosure-norms-put-advertising-through-new-compliance-test-11846073)

### Inferences
- **Gulel's content is SGI under Indian law.** Seedance/Kling photoreal humans, Nano Banana stills and ElevenLabs voices are "algorithmically generated… appears to be real… indistinguishable from a natural person". None of the good-faith exclusions (text, routine edits, accessibility) apply.
- **The legal duty to label and verify SGI falls on the platforms,** as intermediaries/SSMIs: Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, WhatsApp, and likely ShareChat/Moj and Snapchat, given their India scale. That duty flows to Gulel as the uploader: Gulel must answer the SGI declaration truthfully on every upload. A false "no" risks label enforcement, removal and account penalties, and it defeats the purpose now that auto-detection (Meta C2PA/IPTC, YouTube internal signals) is standard.
- **Keep provenance metadata intact.** The rules require platforms not to allow removal of labels or metadata. Gulel's pipeline should *not* strip C2PA/SynthID from generator outputs. If any tool (e.g., Google's Nano Banana) embeds C2PA marking content as fully AI, YouTube's label becomes permanent. That is fine, because the label carries no reach penalty.
- **Character accounts on Instagram must use the "AI-generated profile" label** (Pishachini, CEO hero, substitute bride, mafia lord all feature AI-generated people as the profile subject). Otherwise they lose Reels/Explore recommendations, which the discovery funnel depends on. Whether the *brand* account also needs the profile label is unclear, since its subject is not a single AI person. At minimum it needs the post-level "AI info" disclosure on every photoreal clip.
- **ASCI dual disclosure for character accounts.** They are "virtual influencers" under ASCI's definition and must say upfront and prominently that they are not real humans. Put it in the bio line (e.g., "AI character from Gulel's *Pishachini* | एआई किरदार") and at the start of captions. Where a post promotes anything (Gulel episodes, a partner brand), add ad disclosure as required. If the ASCI AI draft is finalised as written, paid ads would also need "Audio/Video created using AI" on the creative.
- **Recommended belt-and-braces practice** (inference, not a legal requirement for a content publisher's own organic posts): burn a small on-screen "AI-generated / एआई द्वारा निर्मित" watermark into all social cuts. Include a spoken or on-screen disclosure early in audio-only assets. This matches the spirit of the IT Rules' prominence standard and the dropped 10% draft rule, and it pre-empts ASCI.
- **Impersonation risk.** AI faces must not resemble real actors, influencers or public figures. YouTube's likeness detection (all creators 18+) lets a real person who resembles a character request removal. The 3-hour takedown window under the IT Rules makes platform response to complaints fast.

## Q5. Community management for character accounts: comment replies, polls, "choose what happens next", fan theories, character-to-character collabs, live sessions with AI avatars

### Takeaway
No sourced evidence was gathered on community-management tactics (search budget exhausted). The only sourced constraints are regulatory and platform ones. ASCI requires virtual influencers to disclose upfront that users are not interacting with a real human. Instagram's Collab feature is the sanctioned way to co-post between accounts, which suits character-to-character interactions.

### Cited Findings
- ASCI: a virtual influencer "must additionally disclose to consumers that they are not interacting with a real human being", upfront and prominently — [ASCI guidelines PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/GUIDELINES-FOR-INFLUENCER-ADVERTISING-IN-DIGITAL-MEDIA.pdf)
- Instagram's Collab (co-author) feature credits both accounts and sends reach and engagement to the original post rather than to reposts — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Kyra's creators aimed for her to "create content autonomously" using AI trend analysis. This suggests Indian virtual-influencer operators are moving toward semi-automated persona management — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/neon/social-buzz/kyra-meta-influencer-indias-first-robot-influencer)

### Inferences
- **Character-to-character play:** use Collab posts for in-world interactions (e.g., the mafia lord "threatening" the CEO hero; the substitute bride replying to a rival). This keeps one original post per interaction and avoids the originality penalties in Q3. Comment-section banter between character accounts can extend storylines between episodes.
- **Comment replies in character:** because of ASCI's "not a real human" principle, any in-character reply, DM auto-responder or live session should sit under a clearly disclosed AI-character identity (bio label, pinned post, profile label). An in-character reply must never claim to be a real person. In a live session with an AI avatar, show a persistent on-screen "AI character" tag.
- **Interactive formats:** "choose what happens next" polls (Stories polls, YouTube community polls), fan-theory prompts and "which character are you" quizzes give strong comment and poll signals. They also produce new, original assets each time, which helps originality ranking. Poll outcomes could feed actual episode or "alternate ending" Shorts.
- **Horror-specific care** (Pishachini: The Healer): healer/witch framing touches on real-world beliefs, e.g. that "healers" and witchcraft practices exist. Community moderation should avoid content that could read as endorsing real "cures". This is an inference and was not researched.
